A. Ethyne and sp hybridisation

Question 1

Which statement correctly describes the structure of ethyne (C₂H₂)?

Ethyne is linear: H–C≡C–H, with one hydrogen on each carbon and a carbon–carbon triple bond.

Question 2

In ethyne (C₂H₂), the two carbons are joined by a triple bond consisting of one σ bond and two π bonds.

The C≡C triple bond contains one σ bond along the internuclear axis and two π bonds, perpendicular to each other.

Question 3

Which statement is true of sp hybridisation?

In sp hybridisation, one s orbital and one p orbital give two equivalent hybrid orbitals.

Question 4

In which molecule are there sp-hybridised atoms?

The carbons of ethyne are sp-hybridised; those of methane are sp³ and those of ethene are sp².

Question 5

What angle do the two sp hybrid orbitals form?

The two sp orbitals point in opposite directions: a linear arrangement, 180°.

Question 6

sp hybrid orbitals have a higher energy than the original s orbital.

An sp orbital has 50% s character and 50% p character; its energy is higher than that of s and lower than that of p.

Question 7

Which statement best explains why π bonds are weaker than σ bonds?

Sideways overlap is smaller than end-on overlap; smaller overlap means a weaker bond.

Question 8

Which statement correctly describes the effect of sp hybridisation on the geometry of the molecule?

The two sp orbitals point in opposite directions, so the atoms bonded to them lie on a straight line (180°).

B. σ and π bonds and hybridisation

Question 9

In the HCl molecule, the bond forms by overlap of:

The 1s orbital of H overlaps axially with the half-filled 3p orbital of Cl: an s–p σ bond.

Question 10

The triple bond in the N₂ molecule consists of:

There is only one σ bond between two atoms; the other two overlaps are sideways, so they are two π bonds.

Question 11

In sp² hybridisation of carbon, the orbitals mixed are:

The superscript shows how many p orbitals take part: sp² = one 2s + two 2p → 3 hybrids; one 2p orbital remains unhybridised.

Question 12

Which statement about hybrid orbitals is correct?

n atomic orbitals of the same atom give n equivalent hybrids; these form only σ bonds.

Question 13

Addition of H₂ to ethene gives ethane. The hybridisation of the C atoms changes:

In ethene, each C has 3 σ bonds (sp²); in ethane, it has 4 single bonds, that is, 4 σ bonds (sp³).

C. True or false?

Select T if the statement is true or F if it is false.

1. In the H₂ molecule, the bond is a σ bond and forms by s–s overlap.

The two 1s orbitals overlap along the axis.

2. A π bond can form by overlap of an s orbital with a p orbital.

A π bond forms only by sideways overlap of parallel p orbitals.

3. Every single covalent bond is a σ bond.

The first bond between two atoms is always a σ bond.

4. The unhybridised p orbital of an sp² carbon lies in the plane of the sp² orbitals.

It is perpendicular to the plane of the three sp² orbitals.

5. In ethyne, the two π bonds lie in mutually perpendicular planes.

They are formed from the py and pz orbitals, which are perpendicular to each other.

6. In ethane, the C–H bonds form by sp²–1s overlap.

In ethane, the carbons are sp³: sp³–1s overlap.
