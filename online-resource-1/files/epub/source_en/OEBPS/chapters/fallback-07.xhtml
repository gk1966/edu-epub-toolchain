Exploring σ and π bonds in full: static version

This page gives the same information as the interactive 3D model, for reading applications that do not run 3D content.

Linear ethyne molecule, H–C≡C–H, with a bond angle of 180° (sp hybridisation). In total, the molecule has 3 σ bonds (1 C–C and 2 C–H) and 2 π bonds. The C≡C triple bond consists of 1 σ + 2 π; the two pairs of p orbitals that form the π bonds have mutually perpendicular axes. The model shows the σ-bond regions, the sp hybrid lobes, the two π bonds and two perpendicular planes containing the axis.

Parts of the model

  • σ-bond regions. Regions of end-on (axial) overlap along the internuclear axis. Ethyne has three σ bonds: one C–C and two C–H.
  • sp hybrid orbitals. Lobes of the sp hybrid orbitals. Each carbon atom has two, in a straight line at 180°. The σ bonds are formed from these.
  • π bonds. The two π bonds, formed by sideways overlap of the unhybridised p orbitals of the two carbons. The two pairs of p orbitals overlap sideways in mutually perpendicular directions and, together with the C–C σ bond, form the triple bond.
  • Plane 1. Contains the C–C axis. It is a symmetry plane of the molecule and also the nodal plane of one of the two π bonds: the π-electron density is zero in this plane. Perpendicular to Plane 2. Shown with a solid outline.
  • Plane 2 (stripes). Contains the C–C axis. It is a symmetry plane of the molecule and also the nodal plane of one of the two π bonds: the π-electron density is zero in this plane. Perpendicular to Plane 1. Shown with stripes and an outline.
  • Hydrogen atoms. The two hydrogen atoms at the ends of the molecule. The molecule is linear: H–C≡C–H.
  • sp and p labels (3D). The letters sp and p as 3D geometry. A screen reader does not read them; the same names appear in this list.
  • x, y, z axes. Coordinate axes. The molecular axis is the C–C axis.
