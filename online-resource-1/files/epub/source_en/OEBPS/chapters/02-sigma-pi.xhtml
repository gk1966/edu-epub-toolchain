σ and π bonds: orbital overlap

According to valence bond theory, a covalent bond forms when two atomic orbitals, each containing one unpaired electron, overlap. The two electrons, with opposite spins, form a shared pair.

Key principle: the greater the overlap of the orbitals, the stronger the bond.

The σ bond

A σ bond forms by overlap of orbitals along the axis joining the nuclei (end-on overlap). The electron cloud of the bond is symmetrical about the axis. It can result from s–s, s–p or p–p overlap, and also from overlap of hybrid orbitals.

The π bond

A π bond forms by sideways overlap of p orbitals that are parallel to each other and perpendicular to the bond axis. Its electron cloud consists of two regions, one above and one below the axis.

Try it: bring the atoms closer together

The orbitals do not overlap yet.

  • Sideways overlap is smaller than overlap along the axis; this is why the π bond is weaker than the σ bond.
  • A π bond forms only between atoms that are already joined by a σ bond: it exists only in double and triple bonds.

Note: a π bond is not “two bonds”. The two regions of the electron cloud (above and below) make up one π bond, with one shared pair of electrons.
