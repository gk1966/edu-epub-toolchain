Section 6

Misconceptions to correct

The following ideas are common among students studying chemical bonding and hybridisation (Nakhleh, 1992; Nicoll, 2001; Salame et al., 2022). Read each misconception and its correction.

Misconception: “A π bond is two bonds, because it has two regions.”

Correct: The two regions (above and below the axis) belong to one π bond, with one shared pair of electrons.

Misconception: “A triple bond is three identical bonds.”

Correct: It is 1 σ + 2 π. The σ bond results from end-on sp–sp overlap, the π bonds from sideways p–p overlap. They are not equivalent.

Misconception: “Hybrid orbitals also form π bonds.”

Correct: Hybrid orbitals form only σ bonds. π bonds come from p orbitals that have not been hybridised.

Misconception: “Hybridisation takes place between orbitals of different atoms.”

Correct: Orbitals of the same atom are mixed. The overlap between orbitals of different atoms is the bond.

Misconception: “sp orbitals have the same energy as 2s.”

Correct: Their energy is intermediate between 2s and 2p (50% s character, 50% p character).

Misconception: “The colours of the lobes indicate charge.”

Correct: In the figures and the 3D models, the colours distinguish groups of orbitals. They do not indicate electric charge.

Misconception: “The boundaries of the lobes are hard surfaces.”

Correct: The boundaries simply enclose the region where the electron is most likely to be found. The models are simplifications.
