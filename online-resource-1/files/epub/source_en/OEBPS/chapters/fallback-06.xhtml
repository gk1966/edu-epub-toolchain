Axis and symmetry planes of ethyne: static version

This page gives the same information as the interactive 3D model, for reading applications that do not run 3D content.

Linear ethyne molecule, H–C≡C–H, with a bond angle of 180° (sp hybridisation). In total, the molecule has 3 σ bonds (1 C–C and 2 C–H) and 2 π bonds. The C≡C triple bond consists of 1 σ + 2 π; the two pairs of p orbitals that form the π bonds have mutually perpendicular axes. The molecule belongs to the D∞h point group: rotation by any angle about the C∞ axis leaves it unchanged. The model shows the two sp lobes, the molecular axis, two C₂ axes and three symmetry planes. D∞h has infinitely many C₂ axes perpendicular to C∞ and infinitely many σv planes; the model shows a few of them as examples.

Parts of the model

  • sp lobes. Lobes of the sp hybrids: each carbon has two sp hybrid orbitals pointing in opposite directions (180°), which give the linear geometry.
  • Molecular axis C∞. The principal axis. Rotation by any angle about it leaves the molecule unchanged.
  • Other axes. Two of the infinitely many C₂ axes: perpendicular to the molecular axis, passing through the midpoint of C≡C.
  • σh plane. Symmetry plane perpendicular to the C∞ axis, at the midpoint of the C≡C bond. Solid fill.
  • σv1 plane (stripes). Symmetry plane containing the C∞ axis. It is also the nodal plane of one π bond.
  • σv2 plane (dots). A second plane containing the axis, perpendicular to σv1. It is the nodal plane of the other π bond.
